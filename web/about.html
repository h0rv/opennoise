How the atlas is built

OpenNoise is a music genre map and similar-artist explorer inspired by Every Noise at Once, created by Glenn McDonald. OpenNoise is not affiliated with Every Noise or Spotify. Every Noise genre names and artist memberships are used as training labels.

How it’s built

  1. Listening graph. ListenBrainz user–artist counts produce TF-IDF cosine neighbours and ALS listening vectors. Discogs styles rerank similar artists.
  2. Genres. Every Noise memberships propagate through the listening graph; Leiden clustering finds additional groups at three resolutions.
  3. Naming. Wikidata genres and Discogs styles name clusters. A separate model combines places and styles into micro-genres.
  4. Map and Islands. UMAP projects ALS and AcousticBrainz vectors. The Map is oriented with audio descriptors, rank-spread and gently repelled; Islands uses a separate UMAP fit.
  5. Export. Names, memberships, coordinates and neighbours become a static JSON catalog. The browser loads it and requests Deezer previews on demand.

Results

  • Hidden Every Noise genres with a matching cluster: 210 of 868 (24.2%).
  • With artists’ Wikidata labels held back, 98 of 262 genres have a matching cluster (37.4%). A match means at least 20% intersection over union of artists.
  • Top-ten similar artists sharing a hidden Every Noise label: 21.93% with Discogs styles, versus 21.48% listening-only.
  • The 2D map keeps about 42% of each genre’s ten nearest listening-and-audio neighbours.
